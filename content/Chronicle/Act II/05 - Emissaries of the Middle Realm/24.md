## Of the Climbing of the Tree, and of What Was Asked of them

The city received them as guests and they went through it with the attention guests pay a place they had not previously believed to be real. They walked the four islands and crossed the arches that should not have stood, and took meals at long tables, and learned enough of [[tengu]] manners to commit only the more forgivable errors, and trained beside [[tengu]] warriors who corrected their footwork with more honesty than any human teacher would have offered them. [[Shi'rokha]] walked them through the older quarters, where his people had built among inheritances, and spoke of his goddess as a captain speaks of a queen he serves and would also follow into a private war — with respect, and with the wariness owed a power one lives near. She would receive them when she was disposed to. They were welcome to wait.

They climbed the Great Sky-Weaver Tree, which is hollow within, a mile of spiral path cut into the inner bark for those without wings, the light coming through the wood in panes. The climb took the better part of a day. At the top the canopy opened into a platform the tree had grown for the purpose, ringed about by branches bent like a wall against the high wind, and at its centre stood a hoop of living wood grown upright, framing nothing that the eye could see. They sat at its edge and waited. Hours passed. _Goddesses keep their own time,_ they said, in the tenth hour or the twelfth, learning the lesson that country teaches its faithful. Then the frame was not empty. [[Amanozako]] came through it first, in robes of red and black falling in folds the wind did not touch, and her face was the face of every [[tengu]] carving ever chiselled and was plainly the original of which those carvings are copies. Princess of the Tengu, and their goddess, and granddaughter of [[Fujin]]. She inclined her head to her people first and to her guests after. Behind her came an old woman in pale greens and pinks, and [[Okamura Renjishi|Guroutsu]] knew her before any introduction was offered: [[The Summer Queen|Shion]], the [[miko|shrine maiden]] who had spoken to him at a wayside shrine in Kosetsu and turned him south, in the days before any of this. [[Amanozako]] named her [[the Summer Queen]].

Prophecies were spoken there, older than the country the six walked. The shape of the promised saviour was the shape of six, and the six were not difficult to recognise when those two spoke it. They were named Emissaries of the [[Middle Realm]]. The [[Takamagahara|divine realms]], the goddesses said, and the [[fey courts]], and the distant planes, were fractured — that was their word — and could not descend into the mortal world to act directly without risking the very calamity such acts would be meant to prevent. The gods could not save. The fey could not intervene. The realms above and beside were full of figures who by their nature could not now do what needed doing, and mortal hands were the only hands that could touch the work. The mandate was simple in shape: go to [[Seiryuuyama]], stand before [[Fujin]], and carry the fragile hope of the [[kami]] forward, there being nothing else left to carry it. More was said than this. It was asked that the rest not be written, and it is not written. They came down out of the tree, and [[Shi'rokha]] saw them to the lower city, and the [[tengu]] sent them off with the practical kindnesses one offers travellers going a long way. By the next morning they were on solid ground and on the road toward [[Fukama]].
