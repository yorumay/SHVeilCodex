## Of What the Old Druid Said, and of the City Above the Cloud

[[Akiyama]] received them and they told him what they had seen. He gave them the shape of it, so far as he had it. The crack in the veil had opened upon [[Yomi-no-Kuni|Yomi]], which is the land of the dead, and the ruin had stood over that crack for a great age and held it shut after a fashion by virtue of its own weight. He and his apprentice had been finding such cracks across [[Kozakura]] for some years. Some they had sealed. None had been of that scale. The ruin had been part of the sealing somehow — _somehow,_ the master said, with the honest somehow of an old man who knows how much two thousand years will bury. Whatever the place had been before it was a stopper was past recovering. Then he gave his apprentice leave, and said the plain things old teachers say to old students at the door of a long road; and to the six he said that he had read them as he had read the boy, and that they stood at the centre of things, which is not a compliment in that country and was not meant as one.

The road up [[Akagane-no-Oka]] was steep, and the place the [[tengu]] had marked was higher than steep makes comfortable, and four trolls had settled about it for reasons of their own. These were put down without much delay, the work being clean by the standard the six had begun to set themselves. At the centre of the place stood old stone with a hollow cut into its top, plainly meant to receive a feather. They set the feather the [[tengu]] had given them into the hollow and spoke the words they had been taught, and waited, and nothing came of it. They spoke them again and waited longer. Hours passed. The wind shifted twice and the cloud above them moved without breaking.

They had begun to discuss going down and trying again in the morning when the cloud opened over them and the [[tengu]] came out of it in number with a palanquin borne among them. They apologised for the delay only so far as Tengu apologise for anything, which is to say hardly at all, and one of them remarked in passing that their pronunciation was horrible. The six got into the palanquin and were carried up. [[Ak'rat]] came into view above the cloud as four islands, each broad enough to hold a city, joined by stone arches that did not appear from beneath to be carrying weight in any sensible way. Every island lay layered with ruin upon ruin, and the [[tengu]] had built among the ruins, fitting their architecture to whatever older work they had inherited. Out of the central island grew a tree a mile in height, its canopy stretched across all four islands and beyond them, dappling the whole city in green light. The Great Sky-Weaver Tree, the [[tengu]] in the palanquin called it, and did not elaborate. The palanquin came down upon the outer island, and they stepped out into the city.
