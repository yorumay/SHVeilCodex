## Of the Name of the Thieves, and of the Thing in the Road

They did not go to the [[lizardfolk]]'s settlement. It had been marked and it would keep; the fan would not. They put it behind them and took the western road. The villages along it gave them the name of those they chased. [[Kazehaya]], robed in murasaki purple, [[ninja]] in the [[shogun]]'s keeping, come into [[Shizume]] some seasons before. They had taken root in the town of Hakuba, and taken root in a manner for which this account has no ordinary words: beneath Hakuba, so the road's reports said. A second town under the first. Tunnels and halls cut where none were watching, and a school of the silent crafts at work in them. Having come west after one theft, the six began to understand that the theft was the least of what such a house does.

Two days short of the town they came upon bodies in the road. Not one, but several, in the purple they now knew. They had been torn — the word is used with care — torn and not cut. The wounds were not the wounds of any duel. The shapes of them were wrong, and the air about them was wrong, and the space between the bodies was wrong. The thing in the road took the halfling first, lifting him off his feet by nothing the eye could find, and the wind about him sharpened and began to take him apart in the air. [[Shinchuu]] reached him before it had finished, and broke the hold, and went up himself in the halfling's place. The others closed upon what they could not see and killed it by feel and by the displacement it made in the air about their blades. The older texts call such a thing an invisible stalker. Both men lived.

What the bodies implied the six did not stay to confirm. The stalker had killed the [[Kazehaya]]; but the stalker had also been trapped before it killed them, for something about it had bent the air as a window-pane bends the light through it. The working had not held, or else it had held and been too generous, and the thing had killed them inside it. Who set it, and for whom, is not written. Later that day they came over a rise and stopped. A wayside shrine stood in a clearing, old work, weather-greyed, its [[kami]] not to be made out at that distance. An [[oni]] knelt before it with hands clasped and head bowed, praying; and from a vessel held against its breast it was pouring a thick black liquid down across the shrine's stone, which moved as oil moves upon cold water, slowly, and with intent. The forest behind the shrine snickered. There were [[gnoll]]s in it, hidden poorly enough to be read from the road before they read the road. The six drew back to where the way bent and the sight-line broke, and began to plan.
