## Of the Hound Upon the Road, and of the Empty Town

They took the [[gnoll]]s first and the [[oni]] after. The demon was not easily put down, but it was alone and they were six, and after a long exchange in which it used every trick its kind keeps it fell to its knees and finished the prayer it had begun before they came. _Mother,_ it said. _Brother._ It died kneeling. The black stuff in its vessel proved on inspection to be blood and ash mixed — old blood, and ash out of a fire they had not seen. The shrine stood where it stood, anointed with a substance whose purpose is not written here. Then a [[Kezef|hound]] came over the rise, and stood in the road.

It was tall. Taller than the wayside shrine; taller than a temple. Its fur was black and thick and matted in the manner of a thing that has not lain down in a long while, and its eyes were pale, and the pale of them was not the pale of weakness but of attention. It saw them across a distance that should have meant safety. It began to run. It ran toward them, and it ran past them, and between those two facts there is no transition that can be set down. It did not arrive. It went through the road as though the road were air and was not upon the road on the other side. They stood there a long minute, breathing. None of them offered a name for it. This account offers none on their behalf.

Hakuba lay over the next rise and the day's work had cost them the light, and they came down on the town at dusk, which favoured them. The town was empty. The word is used with care: not deserted, and not fled — empty. Doors were latched as though for a market day. The public lanterns were lit. A fire burned low in the smithy's forge as if the smith would be back to it. A few old people sat in their houses and smiled and nodded and gave nothing. The working life of the place had gone out of it in good order, and lately enough that the cookfires of an hour before were still warm. They searched it house by house and floor by floor until they found what was meant to be found: a passage down, beneath a building chosen for the purpose, into the under-town. They went down, and every door closed behind them at once, stone upon stone, in the manner of a mechanism set long ago and tested recently. From the grates in the floor a pale mist began to rise, and it was poison. On a low table at the centre of the chamber a note had been left, which is preserved here entire: _Greetings, [[Honda]] intruders. We expected for you to find us here, but unfortunately you are too late. The fan is already ours. It was fun while it lasted, but now you're going to die. Don't take it personally._ It was signed [[Kazehaya Kaito]]. What followed was the examination of a sealed room by people who were being asphyxiated in it — locks that opened upon riddles, mechanisms whose resetting made sense only after they had been worked wrongly once. The apparatus was elegant and had not been built to be beaten by anyone with breath in their lungs. They beat it regardless. The mist stopped. The far doors groaned open into the deeper halls, and above them a voice they had no face for gave the order, calmly, to kill any who survived. They had survived. They set their backs to the wall beside the stair and made themselves small in the dark, and waited for the men coming down to find that the prey was not the prey they had been promised.
