## Of the House Completed, and of the Thing in the Canals

The road south thawed beneath them and [[Fukama]] opened its gates to those who had ended a winter the city had been ready to die in. The lord received them and took the prophecy from their hands and read it again in the room where a monk had died delivering it — the same three lines spoken in that hall half a year before, returned to him by the hands he had sent to read them. He thanked them and gave them their leave. The house was waiting. It had been finished while they were on the mountain: shutters opened, hearth lit, the beams sound and the roof whole. They moved into it properly for the first time and slept beneath their own timbers. It is from this point that they are called [[Shinzatsu]].

The days that followed are of a kind this account has not had cause to keep. Errands. Practice. The lesser works of those who are between one labour and the next — armour mended, blades sharpened against quieter stones, a cabbage carried home from a stall as any household carries one. It is written here because not every day is a descent into a mountain, and a record that keeps only the descents will lie about the life.

Then a rumour, of the kind a city makes when it cannot name its fear. People had been going missing along the canals, late, on the walks home from the teahouses; one the week before, another the week before that, never enough at once to bring the watch out in force, and always near the water. They went to look. What was in the canals was not what the rumour had imagined. Tentacles came out of the dark and took [[Hisakawa Fuurin]] off his feet before the others had drawn breath, and the halfling went in after him and was taken the same way, and the ink it threw across the water hid them both at once. They fought it half-blind in cold water by the rails of a stone walk built for lovers, and killed it, and brought both men up in the same minute. Then they hauled the thing to the bank and broke it down by lantern-light, and when the teahouses began emptying their drunker patrons into the street there was a cookfire on the canal and takoyaki coming off it in batches. So the city was fed upon the thing that had been eating it. [[Fukama]] had cheered them once for the head of an [[oni]]. It cheered them again for octopus fried in batter on the canal road.
