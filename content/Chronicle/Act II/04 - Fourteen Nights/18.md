## Of the Empty Temple, and of the Road Toward the Copper Hills

They began at the [[Toro-dai temple]], where the fan had been set out for viewing through the festival's first nights. The chamber was beautiful and the chamber was empty. The [[tengu]] samurai who had kept the watch there — around the clock, in shifts, in numbers that should have made the thing impossible — were alive. All of them. This is the first matter the account sets down, because it was the first matter the six remarked upon: the thieves had not killed. They had gone through a guard of warriors and left those warriors breathing. Whether by skill, or by intention, or by some constraint not yet read, no [[tengu]] died for the fan to leave that room, and such restraint is not shown by accident. The one thing left behind was a scrap of cloth snagged upon the high beam through which the relic had gone out — dark stuff, finely woven, dyed in a purple that the lantern-light did not flatter. They took it and went after the rest.

The trail was not subtle. Whatever the fan had done when it opened in the thieves' hands had plainly not been intended. Roofs were torn along the line of its flight and lantern poles were down, and the damage cut out through the festival quarters and past the city wall into the country beyond. They followed the wreckage as far as it ran and then provisioned themselves to follow further, and took the road toward [[Akagane-no-Oka]]. The country between is swampland in spring — old water and slow water, water that warms early and thaws older things out of its silt. They were half a day out when the [[lizardfolk]] became a matter.

In ordinary years the [[lizardfolk]] of that country are no great matter. They take what their numbers require and they are gone. That year they had come up out of the swamps in numbers, and hungry, and with a boldness no map of the district had record of. They had taken a [[Bakufu]] checkpoint upon the road, killed its garrison, broken its gate, and were pulling it apart for what might be carried home. The six came upon them in the middle of the looting. The garrison was past saving; the lizardfolk were not. It is set down here that these six, who had no love for the [[Bakufu]], fought for its dead regardless. The road carries its dead as it carries everyone else, and they chose not to leave those bodies unanswered. When the work was finished they rested, and the halfling went on alone a while, being small and quick and having that people's instinct for going where larger eyes do not look. He found the [[lizardfolk]]'s settlement upon the shore of a swamp-lake some hours off the road, and marked its place, and came back and told them what he had seen of it.
