## The Seventeenth Record - Of the Coming of the Tengu, and of the Theft of the Fan

The week did not begin with politics. It began with drums upon the temple steps and lanterns going up along the canal walks and the smell of grilled food in every quarter of the city. The [[Festival of Divine Descent]] runs its full ten days from the eleventh of Ches to the twentieth, and the twentieth is the day upon which the first Emperor took the throne, one thousand four hundred and fifty-nine years before. [[Fukama]] does not sleep through those nights, and neither did they. On the third day the [[tengu]] came. They came down out of [[Ak'rat]], which the maps of human [[Kozakura]] mark only as a notation, hidden in the copper hills that none walk without invitation; and they came robed in the high colours of their kind, and the lord asked the six to receive them at the gate and bring them to his hall. He who led them greeted [[Honda Katsuhiro]] as old friends greet one another after long absence. It is recorded here only that the friendship between [[Shizume]] and [[Ak'rat]] is older than that lord's tenure, and that the two had walked through the door of it before. The days that followed passed in colour and are not set down at length.

The talks began upon the fifth day, and the six were admitted as witnesses, which was a courtesy and a favour and an acknowledgement of what they had done in the north. The matter was a formal alliance: the [[tengu]] of [[Ak'rat]] and the people of [[Shizume]], sworn together against the [[Bakufu]]. It is from this hour that the account ceases to concern a lord's monsters and begins to concern a province's posture. Then the doors broke open. Two [[tengu]] who were no part of the retinue came through them at speed and pointed across the chamber and said: _Those are the adventurers. They stole the fan._ The [[tengu]] had brought their dynastic relic with them, the Fan of Fujin, which is the breath of the wind itself and marks the right to rule over their people, and it had been taken in the night, and the faces of the six had been seen at the taking.

What followed were hours of a kind this account does not enjoy keeping. They had no motive. They had an alibi. They had no means. The witnesses were certain of what they had seen; the chamber was certain the witnesses had seen something; and the two certainties stood in the room and did not agree. So the matter was given to the Lady [[Jiko-no-Tan]], who is an ancient [[hengeyokai]] of the crane line and among the last [[onmyoji]] in the realm to have taken her initiation under the [[Imperial Rites]]. What she does is read [[the Weave]] directly, and the ripples upon the surface of magic become light under her hand, so that what has happened in a place may be made to play again in the air, as water leaves its print on stone. She read the night the fan was taken. The thieves who entered the temple wore the faces of the six — not approximations, and not resemblances, but faces — and yet the seeming was not perfect, and there were small things wanting in it. She dimmed the light. The chamber understood, and the witnesses understood last and most painfully: they had been right and wrong in the same act. They had seen [[Shinzatsu]]. [[Shinzatsu]] had not been there. The work was named. Find the thieves, recover the relic, and return both honour and fan to their proper hands. The chamber emptied, and the festival went on outside without its noise.
