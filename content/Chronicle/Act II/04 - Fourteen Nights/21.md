## Of the Two Letters, and of the Nameless Place in the Hills

The killers came down the stair into the wrong room. The six let them come, and waited until they had committed to the chamber, and closed on them from where the [[Kazehaya]] had not thought to look. What followed was not clean. They had come prepared as that house prepares — poison on every blade, smoke for every angle of withdrawal, traps thrown across the floor in the instant a foot was set to it. Almost every one of the six went down at least once, and almost every one was lifted from where he fell by another. This is written as the nearest they had yet come to losing the work, and the outcome is owed as much to fortune as to skill. _Such is fate,_ they said afterward, by the wall, breathing. _We have been blessed._ It is not for this account to contradict them.

Two letters were found upon the bodies, both written for carrying and both sealed in haste. The first said: _The so-called heroes of [[Fukama]] have arrived in Hakuba. Make sure they don't get out._ Its seal was golden wax bearing a falcon. The device is set down here; the house it belongs to is not, for it was not then known. The second said: _We have the fan bound. Masaki and Kenta are dead. Cut apart by the wind. Awaiting further orders._ With it was a map drawn hastily and only indicative, marking a place south of the town in the copper hills, in country the six had not been sent to walk. They took the letters and the map and left the town as they found it, and did not trouble the old people for explanations.

The map brought them to ruins from which no name could be read. The stone was older than the script that century used for such things, and the carving had taken weather since that script was last spoken aloud. Some places lose their names because the world has ceased to need them, and this was such a place. In the centre of it a circle had been cut into the broken floor, and the work was fresh, the cuts still pale where the chisel had bitten, and runes ran about its rim and burned in the dark. A hundred feet above the circle a sphere of stilled air held the Fan of Fujin. The binding was holding. They had found it, and the [[Kazehaya]] found them in the same moment, coming out of every shadow the ruins had been hoarding — across the broken stones, down from the standing walls, up out of places beneath the floor that no one had told the floor it had. The fight was long and hard and twice they believed it won, and each time they believed it more came. The ruins had been expecting them. The binding had been bait as much as resting-place.
