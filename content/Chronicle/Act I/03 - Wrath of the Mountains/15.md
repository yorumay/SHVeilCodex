## Of the Woman in the Ice, and of the Kami That Was Made

The stair went deeper than the building above it should have allowed. The mountain was hollow, a cone widening as it fell, and [[Hoshikage]] had been only the fortified mouth of it. The first floor down had been a home — shelves, a hearth long cold, the corners of a life lived by two people — glazed now in pale blue ice a century thick. Beyond its railing the hollow opened, and hundreds of feet below a single column of crystal rose from the floor of the cone, and within the column was a woman. The way down was a spiral cut into the wall, and the [[yuki-no-ko|snow-children]] went ahead of them upon it, never turning, never closing.

The wind in that place had words. At each level of the descent it gave another verse of a lament, eight in all, which is set down in full elsewhere and will not be repeated here. It told how she had taken the cold as her office and the lost as her children; how [[Izuna|one]] came to her with stars upon his breath and asked her not to die; how [[Akujin|fire]] fell upon the mountain and they fought it; how [[Hoshikage|men]] came in haste and did not ask, and sealed the bride, and left the god, and swore the storm was dead; how he came to her at the last on shattered wings and broke beneath their trust; and how those who followed took the [[obsidian heart|heart]] that might have freed her and left her his crumbling shape to mourn over. By the eighth verse they had reached the floor. Before the column lay what remained of [[Izuna]] — bone and stiff cloth, his sword still in his hand, his arm still reaching toward the ice. He had crawled that far in his last breath. He had not reached her.

The [[yuki-no-ko|children]] reached her first. They climbed the column and beat their small fists against it without sound until the ice that had held a hundred years gave way. [[Fuyukimi|She]] stepped out, and the wind took shape behind her, and the six stood in the open between a god in mourning and the man upon the floor. They did not raise weapons. They spoke to her — of her [[Izuna|husband]], of what the monks had done and had not understood, of the [[obsidian heart|heart]] that had been meant for her and was broken three days before. What was said is not written. It is written that they said it gently, and that the storm went still. Then what Izuna had become passed through their words and reached her, and she knelt, and the tears that fell had been frozen a hundred years. _I am so tired,_ she said. Light rose in the cone and the two went up together within it, and a door opened, and beyond it two children were waiting. She looked back once. _Thank you,_ she said, _for ending my winter._ The door closed in aurora and falling snow, and the cone was warm. So a [[kami]] was made upon that mountain. They climbed out and went south, and the road home was warmer than the road north had been, and shorter, and quiet.
