## Of the Winter That Came Early, and of the Children in the River

The cold came upon [[Fukama]] in earnest in the days after the spider's death. Snow that had been a rumour out of the high country came down into the valleys, and then into the streets, and then through doorframes that had never known frost. Crops froze where they stood. The lord had sent envoys north to the monastery of [[Hoshikage]], from which prophecy in that part of the world was made, and they did not return. He called the six to him and braided three errands into one: find the envoys, reach the monastery, and bring back what could be brought back of the meaning of the words. He had read them himself and read them as a warning of war — [[Bakufu|shogunate]] against throne, the divine mandate undone in ash, the seat of heaven shaking above an earthly quarrel.

They were leaving by the river gate when the river rose against them. Children came up out of the water in white. These were [[yuki-no-ko]], the snow-children, of whom every village in [[Shizume]] tells some story. They did not turn aside. They went north, and those who stood in their way did not survive the standing. The six fought hard and halted the children at last, and more than a dozen were dead in the market before it was done.

The road north was colder than it had been. They came to [[Hoshizuki]] by dusk and the town's hot springs had never been more welcome, and they lay in the steam with their weapons set aside, and the cold went out of their bones for the first time in days. Then there was laughter in the frozen trees — high, and breaking, and not a child's — and [[mephit]]s of ice came down upon them out of the branches. Naked and unarmed and wrapped in towels and steam, they reached for whatever was at hand.
