## Of the Village Beneath the Basalt, and of the House at the Lake

The wolves came back in the dark, not as wolves but as carcasses, with blue light in the marrow and blue light in the empty sockets, the cold that had killed them lifting them up off the snow. They made no sound. The six fought them where they had butchered them and put them down a second time, and stood the rest of that night by the fire understanding that they had not been alone in the clearing. Morning showed them why. Beneath the drifts, and beneath a layer of old basalt the drifts had buried, were timbers and foundations and the shapes of houses that had stood there once — a village underneath, taken in a sheet of fire long enough ago that the basalt had had time to weather. Something had come out of [[Izunayama]] within the memory of the people who built there. They had not received the rites. The wolves had been the refusal of them.

Higher, the road met a chasm, and the rope bridge that had crossed it was a streamer in the wind. They rigged lines and went over by hand. A [[yeti]] came out of the cave on the far side when the first of them was already across, and its mate came out of the trees behind them, and the crossing became a battle fought across two surfaces and a fall. They held both ends, and when it was finished both beasts were dead. The cave was where the envoys had ended. Their packs had survived and the message they carried was still in its case, sealed and dry. They themselves had not. The yetis had hunted that crossing as long as the bridge had been broken, and the envoys were the last living thing to come through it. The packs were carried out. The bones were left where they lay.

The road climbed once more and broke, and they came out into stillness. The storm was on every side and there was no storm there. A frozen lake lay flat in a silent bowl, and on the near shore a wooden house with a boat tied at its dock, of the kind that carries pilgrims across in better weather; and on the far shore, just visible over the ice, [[Hoshikage]]. They went to the house. The lights in its windows did not behave as candle-light behaves. The household had been dead some months and was sitting where it had died. When the door opened, the lights came out.
