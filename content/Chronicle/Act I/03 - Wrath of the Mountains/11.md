## Of the Coming to Hoshikage, and of the Name That Was Not in the Count

The lights came out at them and they fought them on the threshold of a house that had been dead for months. These were [[will-o'-the-wisp]]s, of the sort that belong to the cold. They put them down one by one, and afterward there was nothing left in the house but a family in their chairs and the silence the wisps had been keeping for them. The lake carried them across, ice underfoot the whole way, perfectly held and perfectly silent, and by the far shore [[Hoshikage]] had grown from a shape into a building and the building had grown a wall. The palisade ringed the monastery in raw timber and the men upon its walks had the look of men who have been counting nights too long. The eye of the storm drew in about them by inches each day. They could feel it, and had told one another so.

The monks admitted them and gave them the courtesy owed to a lord's envoys. The abbot [[Gen'un]] received them himself, and was stern, and was persuasive, and was plainly the man holding the place together by will alone. A sister of the house gave them its history: raised in honour of [[Izuna]], [[kami]] of stars and of prophecies, and kept by readers who turned the heavens into warnings for the world beneath. But the abbot's assistant met them with a smile too wide for the cold she stood in, and spoke of [[Akujin]] as one might speak of a lover — the god of warmth, she said, the god of fire — and watched their faces for the answer she expected. They did not give it. They had been raised on the eight million kami and could count the gods of fire among them, and [[Akujin]] was not in the count. The name itself is a warning to any who pause over it. _Evil Person._ That a house raised for a [[kami]] of stars now kept a faith devoted to a thing so named was a fact they carried through the rest of that day and did not set down.

The monastery was divided. Some spoke [[Izuna]]'s name with reverence. Others spoke as though he had been a story their ancestors needed and they did not. The abbot held both in the same hall, and the holding was visible work. By nightfall the six had their bearings and nothing else. No one could be spared to them; the defenders were stretched thin, and the libraries — several, and each holding more books than a city's worth — would have to be searched by their own hands. They began to plan. The bells went before the planning was done. The palisade had broken, and the snow-children were within.
