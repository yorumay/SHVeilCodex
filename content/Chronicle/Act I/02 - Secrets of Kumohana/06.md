## Of the Second Temple, and of the Diary Found Within It

The trail led where they had not wished to look: back into the spider-haunted woods and deeper, past the cave, past the places the village's children were told never to go. The temple that stood there had been swallowed in another manner than the first. No earthquake had taken it. The rot had been patient. Webs lay thick upon every gate and doorpost and the air smelled of old incense and of older blood.

They took the monks' quarters first, building by building, killing more spiders than could be counted, and found the altars wrong upon every wall — [[Kumo-no-Kami]] set where other names had stood. Whatever the house had been raised to honour was honoured there no longer. In a small cell at the back of the dormitory was a diary in the hand of the last monk who had kept faith. Three months of entries. The shrine maiden had come to the temple slowly and then quickly. Brothers went into the woods and afterward into nothing. Those few who saw what she was spoke too late. The final entry breaks off in the middle of a sentence. The truth had been learned and the truth had not saved them, and the writer died across the page.

The main temple stood yet. Whatever had been at its lessons for two hundred years was within, and the elder's daughter with it, and they had not come so far to leave the work half done. They went to the door.
