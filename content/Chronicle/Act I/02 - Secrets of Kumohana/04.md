## Of the Return to Fukama, and of the Snow at Kumohana

They came down from the mountain in the morning and into [[Fukama]] by afternoon, the head of the [[shiro-oni]] carried between them. The lord received them formally, with witnesses, in the proper hall, as a lord receives those who have done what was asked of them. A [[roku]] was set against the provincial coffers. Ground was broken in the city for a house to be raised to their measure - [[Gintaka-no-ie]], the house of the silver hawk.

The celebration was short. A monk came through the lord's door out of the north with snow upon his robes that had not had time to thaw, and three steps into the hall he spoke:

> _Divine light fades out. Gaia burns in endless flame. Heaven's throne crumbles._

He took a fourth step and was dead before he reached the floor. The lord bade them rest, saying that he would carry the words himself and would call for them when he had need of them. Weeks passed among carpenters and the raising of beams.

Then the call came. There was to be a festival of the first snow at [[Kumohana]], where the [[shiro higanbana]] grow that grow nowhere else, which are the town's name and its prosperity both and are said to be the gift of [[Kumo-no-Kami]]. _Stand for me at the rite,_ the lord said, and they went. The [[miko|shrine maiden]] [[Akari]] tended the harvest; the elder of the town walked the rows with his daughter beside him. The flowers were gathered. The first snow fell when it was meant to. That night men came out of the dark for food and for supplies and for the potions distilled from the lilies, and ran when steel was drawn; and they followed them out of the lantern-light and into the trees, where the snow held the tracks a while and then gave them up.
