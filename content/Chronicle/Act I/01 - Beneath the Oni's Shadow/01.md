## Of the Road North, and of the Child at Kinzan

They went north from [[Fukama]] in the autumn light, and there were bandits upon that road. These they put down before the day was out, and came to Kumatake carrying arms that were not theirs. The smith Taro received the blades and showed some surprise in receiving them - receiving his own blades back. The innkeeper Yumi gave them a room beside her fire. The first night upon the road was quiet, and there is nothing further to record of it.

By the morning the way climbed towards [[Hoshizuki]], which lies in a river valley and is a town of mines. Its markets cries no fish. The stalls there call prices for iron and for tin, for jade rough from the cut, and a thumbnail of silver weighed against rice. They passed through slowly. [[Hoshizuki]] keeps its prosperity close. North of it the road narrows and grows cold.

On the day following they came to [[Kinzan]], and there they found the child. She was [[korobokuru]], and she was alone, and villagers had brought her down out of the high country not knowing what else might be done for her. Kurotani was her home. She had walked out of it in the night with the [[oni]]'s shadow behind her, and the village of Shimizu had kept her long enough to carry her further from the thing that hunted. At [[Kinzan]] she was warm and she was fed and she was very small. What she knew of the oni she gave them in pieces, as a child gives such things. Until that hour the villages of the north had a rumour only. From that hour they had a witness.
