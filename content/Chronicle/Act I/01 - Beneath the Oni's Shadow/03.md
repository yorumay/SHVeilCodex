## Of the Temple in the Ravine, and of the Kami That Was Forgotten

They lay in the dark above the camp and listened. The [[bakemono]] spoke of a new god, a god of [[goblin]]s, who had given them shelter and prosperity and food enough for the winter; and the temple in the ravine was his gift to them. Hearing this, they drew their conclusions and their blades, and went down. The first hall held a statue of a [[kami]] whose name was already lost, and goblin marks had been scrawled across stone that had been smooth for centuries. Those who guarded it did not last long.

Beneath the temple lay the quarters of the monks, older by ages than the floor above them, and there a hundred years of buried silence had kept what it was given: offerings dried to dust, tools long past their use, and shrines yet unmarred where old magic had been laid against decay and never lifted. The [[kami]] had been well loved once. Lower still a spring had broken into the halls and half-drowned them, and they went waist-deep through the cold water, and the dead came against them - [[gaki]], which had been the monks themselves, dead a century and soured by a passing without rites. These they put down a second time, and went deeper.

The deepest sanctum had belonged to [[Bokyo]], who is the kami of forgetting. In other years people came there to lay down what they could no longer carry: broken tools, broken memories, the names of the dead. What waited in the sanctum appeared first as a great [[ao-oni|blue oni]] dancing upon the air to a [[shakuhachi]] that played itself, and when the seeming fell away there stood a small white thing in kimono, no taller than a woman. [[shiro-oni]], the weakest of that kind. Its illusions were true illusions and its claws were true claws, and twice the fight was nearly lost. It was laughter that took her in the end, and they finished what the laughter began. They took her head. The way back rose through the cold water and the quiet halls and they walked it without speaking, and the moon was up before Shimizu's hearths were near. They slept before they came down the mountain.
