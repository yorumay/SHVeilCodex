## Of the Summons at Fukama

In the autumn of the year 1384, the lord of [[Fukama]] set his seal upon a summons and had it posted at the fortress gates and upon the shrine boards of the province. Warriors were wanted. Some thirty answered. The greater number did not remain, and their names are not written here.

Of the manner of their coming, the tellings differ. [[Hisakawa Fuurin|Fuurin]] of the clan [[Hisakawa]], and [[Leliana]], were already within the city and had spent the last of their coin at a market stall when the notice caught their eyes. [[Shinchuu]] came down from Tamanokuni with the halfling [[Tamara Kiryu|Kiryu]] of the clan [[Tamura]] beside him, the dust of the road still upon them. [[Ishikui Yondai|Yondai]] of the clan [[Ishikui]] came south from [[Tenmei]] by the lesser paths and read the summons at [[Takishiro]]. [[Okamura Renjishi|Renjishi]] of the clan [[Okamura]], going by the name [[Okamura Renjishi|Guroutsu]], came from the north; and it is told - though it is only told - that at a wayside shrine in [[Kosetsu]] a woman met his eye and spoke the summons to him before he had heard of it. He did not know her. He did not ask. He came south the following morning.

When the great daimyo, [[Honda Katsuhiro|Katsuhiro]] of the venerable clan [[Honda]] received them, six remained. To these were added a [[geisha and maiko|maiko]] whose house had broken with her, and a [[ronin]] without a master, and so they were eight. The lord named the work plainly: an oni walked in the villages of the north, and the villages were dying. Find it. End it. They accepted, and left the following morning.
