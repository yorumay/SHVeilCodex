## Of Shimizu, and of What Was Found Beneath the Mountain

Shimizu was still smoking when they came to it. The granary had taken the worst and leaned toward its fall, and the family that founded the village had no time for asking of names - only for hands, and hands they had. The beam was set and hte wall braced before the sun had gone far, and the village's winter was preserved. In this manner they were taken into the work without ever having said who they were.

By the fire that night they were told the rest. Other villages had gone before this one, and the pattern of their going was old enough to have a shape. The children of the house said the oni was hiding in the old temple. The elders put this down as child's tale, as elders do. The visitors heard both, and chose. In the morning they went up into the mountains; and the temple did not wish to be found, and what they found instead was Kurotani, or four burned husks that had been Kurotani. A human family had lived there and a [[korobokuru]] family beside them. The earth was still warm beneath the ash. [[Shinchuu]] took a small toy from the wreckage and carried it away, believing it had belonged to the child at [[Kinzan]].

The tracks in the ash were not the tracks they had been promised. Many feet, and not one. Small feet, and not large. These they followed to a crack in the mountainside that opened upon a ravine, and they went down into the cold of it. At the bottom stood a temple, half-swallowed by stone, dropped into the earth by an earthquake a hundred years gone and standing yet. Before it was a camp, and in the camp were [[bakemono]], the eastern kin of [[goblin]]s. They had settled as though the place were theirs.
