Three tellings, and the first of them breaks the world.

This part was supposed to be the road to Seiryuuyama. The six had been named at Ak'rat and sent to Fujin, and everything from that naming to that mountain should have belonged to as single telling. Instead there is a night in the middle of it that no account of that century can go around, and so the road is broken into a before and an after, and the after is walked by people carrying eighty thousand names they could not put down.

What the fire did is not told elsewhere and is not summarised easily. What matters here is that it did not change this errand. The six went south with the same task they had been given above the cloud, through a country that no longer matched its own maps, and killed an occupying lord in his bath along the way because the road ran through his city. Then they climbed the dragon-mountain adn stood before the wind and were told at last what they had actually been named for.

The Ravager of Heavens is loose. Three bound it once, at dawn of the world, and three must bind it again, and the six were told where those three had gone. Everything before this part is prelude to that sentence. Everything after is the consequence of their having agreed.
