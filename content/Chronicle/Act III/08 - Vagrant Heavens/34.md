## Of What the Wind Required, and of What Came Down to Meet Them

The [[drider]]s did not last the morning. Two of them against six who had slept badly and woken angry — the fight was brief, and the road was clear before the dew had gone off it. They climbed. Halfway up the summit road the temple of [[Fujin]] met them, half-buried in what the years had done to it. Its last keeper had left a message behind him in the manner of keepers who know they will not be coming back: brief, and formal, and addressed to whoever should come next. A [[cockatrice]] had taken up nearby and gone mad with its own solitude, and they put it down, and read what the keeper had left them. Higher still the summit altar stood under open sky with its own guardians about it. These they fought, and stood at the altar when it was done.

What the wind required was not blood, and not treasure, and not any deed. It required truth. Each of them spoke to the altar in turn. That they spoke is written. What was said is not, for what is given to the wind belongs to the wind. The altar received them, and the mountain opened, and a road that had not existed above the summit existed then, and they went up it into the cloud.

What came down to meet them was gold before it was anything else — gold scales drinking the last of the evening light, and the blue fire flickering between them like something the beast had swallowed and not finished with. It came out of the higher cloud in a long unhurried arc and it roared, and they felt the roar in the place behind the chest where courage is kept. Two of them did not step back. Then the dragon's eyes found them.
