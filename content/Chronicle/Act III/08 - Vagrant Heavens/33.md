## Of the Beasts Out of Their Own Age, and of the Two Shrines

The road climbed, and what climbed beside it was no longer of this era. A small herd of thunder-beasts met them in the open — heavy in the shoulder, three-horned, of a kind no living [[Kozakura]]n has cause to know — and were calmed there. The druid read them: they were lost, and they were looking for home, and the home they had been made to find did not exist in the world these six walked. They let them go, and the herd went on toward whatever the [[bluefire]] had taken from it. From this it was understood that the fire does not only destroy and remake, but also _brings_, and that things cross the seam which ought not to move at all. It is one of the few plain lessons of that year, and this account keeps it where it can be found.

Higher up, [[gnoll]]s came down upon them out of cliffs that had been waiting to deliver gnolls for longer than anyone present had been alive, twenty of them, under a captain that was a [[demoniac]]. The fight was hard. Two of the six went down once, and two more came near it, and the work ended with the captain surrounded and the pack dead upon the stone. Past that place were two roadside shrines. About the first the thunder-beasts had been circling, seeking what they remembered of it. The second the [[gnoll]]s had defaced, and [[Hisakawa Fuurin]] made the cleansing rite there; and the rites themselves told what the road's tradition held — [[Fujin]] first, the wind kept by his old keepers, and [[Amaterasu]] set beside him later by a generation that wished both faces of heaven held in the same hand.

Toward dark they came into the ruins of the old waystation and made their camp beneath a cherry tree in full bloom. The [[kodama]] of that tree was old and weak and did not speak. [[Hisakawa Fuurin]] made the old rites and [[Leliana]] proposed a gift, and the spirit received them both. In the early hours [[Shinchuu]] and [[Leliana]] saw what had preyed upon the pilgrims of that road in the years when pilgrims still came: two [[drider]]s, coming down the rock toward the fire.
