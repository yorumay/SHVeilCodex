## Of the Last Village, and of the Thing That Came Out of the Lake

The warning came from the resistance before the gates were shut for good, and they took it. They went out through the northern postern beneath the half-shut eye of a guard who had been paid not to see, and by sunrise they were upon the road with [[Kaisei]] a roofline behind them. The way climbed, and by dusk brought them to the [[Last Village]] — an old name, given the place when it had been nothing but a waystation between the lowlands and [[Fujin]]'s temple, and kept afterward when the waystation became a camp. Those there had come up out of [[Kaisei]] in the days the city was tightening. The six sat among them and learned the small honest forms their endurance had taken, which is a thing worth setting down and is not often set down.

At nightfall the mountain spring-lake began to stir. There had been no wind. An [[aboleth]] came up out of it, and nine [[intellect devourer]]s with it, scuttling across the wet stone. The fight was long and it was brutal, and it ended with the [[aboleth]] dead and slipping back down into the water it had risen from, and the devourers killed where they could be cornered. The lake closed over the body and the body went down, deep, and deeper. What such a thing was doing in a spring-lake upon a mountainside, at that hour, in that year, is a question this account cannot answer and will not pretend to.

They told the refugees what could be told, which was little enough. Something in the lake. Watch the water. Do not drink from it tonight. Then they went on, climbing toward [[Fujin]], with the village shrinking behind them and the lake gone perfectly still again.
