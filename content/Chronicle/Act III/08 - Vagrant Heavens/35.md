## Of the Death of Ryuukon, and of the Naming of a Son

The dragon came down gold and the six came up steel, and something had to break. The fight was long and it was brutal. He was fast and the cliffs were narrow, and [[Ryuukon]] had spent the whole of his existence being only what his instincts had made him, having been given nothing else to be. At the end their tactics outlasted his fury and he fell. His last act was to claw open the passage into [[Fujin]]'s realm — one desperate reach toward somewhere else — and his wounds took him after he had crossed it.

The [[dragon pearl]] shattered as his breath stopped. It had been his mother's. It had been taken from her before she could give it to him with his name and his history in it, and it was carried up that mountain unknowing in the hands of the men who killed him. So the line of [[Shinrai]] ended, not in the ambush of forty years before, but there, upon that slope, in that afternoon. It is set down here because no one who was present knew it at the time, and someone should have.

Beyond the door [[Fujin]] received them. What he told them is given here in broad strokes only: the shape of the [[three evils]], and of the [[three gods]] who bound them, and of the prophecies that had been pointing toward six before any of the six were born. And last, the particular truth he had carried longest. He looked at [[Hisakawa Fuurin]] the way a man looks at something he made and has been dreading at a distance, and spoke his son's name. [[Hisakawa Fuurin]] had been many things on the road to that mountain — bard, and heir, and [[hatamoto]], and the theatrical face of a clan that had survived by never showing its real one. He was all of those still. He was also, from that hour, something that could not be un-named. They went out with the god's blessing and with the weight of what they had been told, and with the wind at their backs which had, as is now understood, always been at his.
