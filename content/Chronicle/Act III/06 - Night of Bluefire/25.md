## Of the Night the Sky Broke Over Fukama

They came home and found themselves already home. The things wearing their faces in the [[Gintaka-no-ie]] did not last the first hour, and were [[Bakufu]] work, and had been taken for the six long enough that everything said in that house had been said to the enemy. The days after were given to preparing for the northern road. In those days a stranger found [[Leliana]] alone in the night, and named himself the Twilight Scrivener, and spoke of eras and of the marks that end them, and called her little raven as though he had known her a long time; and he put her down where she stood and left beside her the means of her own saving, having wanted her humbled and not dead. _I find no joy in slaughtering a bird with clipped wings,_ he said, and was gone, and the night gave his name as [[Yugiri no Kurotsuku]]. In those days also [[Shinchuu]] came upon the [[hitokiri]] [[Saito Junichi]] in an alley above the bodies of city guards, and was mocked, and was spared. _Find a real master,_ the man said. _Find a reason to hate._ These things are set down because they happened. What follows is why the year is remembered.

The twenty-ninth of Tarsakh dawned grey and unremarkable, and by midday there was nothing in it worth the keeping. By night there was too much. The sky over [[Fukama]] broke. The word is used with care, for the word is correct: it cracked, and behind the crack there was nothing — not blackness, and not stars, and not the dark of any night that had ever been walked under, but a void that the eye could not look at and afterward remember having seen. Out of the cracks came fire, and the fire was blue, and it was not the blue of any flame that human craft or magic or kami had ever taught anyone to know. It came down upon the city in lines and in rivers and in falling rain. Where it touched stone, the stone went. Where it touched a roof, the roof went. Where it touched a person, this account declines to say what remained, for what remained was not a person and was not a corpse and was not nothing.

Magic itself caught. Workings burned in mid-flight. Charms sewn into clothing burned upon the bodies wearing them. The wards over their own house burned, and they felt them go through their skin. [[Fukama]] screamed at a pitch no city should be able to reach. This account cannot hold that night in the small clear way it has tried to hold every other. It cannot give the streets it would need to give, nor name the dead it would later be required to name. The night was too large, and those who lived through it did not agree afterward on what they had seen, and the tellings differ in every province where the telling survived. What is certain is only this: that upon that night the sky opened over [[Kozakura]], and the fire that came out of it burned things that fire cannot burn.
