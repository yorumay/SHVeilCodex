## Of the Fire Set Upon Its Road, and of What Was found in the Garden

Tenku-jo held its ground against the god of fire. He did not see the six at the first. He came for the wall, and knocked upon it as a child knocks upon a locked door, unhurried and methodical and certain, and they fought him while he did not yet know that they were there, and the work of it was to buy time. In time something in him turned toward them. What followed was harder than anything set down in this account before it. [[Ishikui Yondai]] went down beneath a stroke that took his eye in the falling and would have taken the rest of him had the others been a fraction slower. At the last the god knelt upon one knee and reached up toward the heavens, and the shard that had animated the fire fell out of him. So the two contended, and so it ended, and so he was set upon his proper road.

They went into the castle, and the court was living. Most of it. The lady [[Matsudaira Yasuko|Yasuko]] of the clan [[Matsudaira]], mother to the lord, met them in the receiving-hall as an old woman meets men who have just done a thing she had not been certain men could still do. Beside her sat the lord's daughter, nine years old, who did not weep, because weeping was a luxury that room had not yet earned. The courtiers stood at the edges. The court had survived and was grieving and did not yet know how much. The lord himself they found in the [[Fukiage]] garden at the back of the castle, where he had taken his guards out to meet the fire when it came over the wall. They had stood where they could be seen from the throne hall. They had drawn their weapons and faced outward. The fire had taken them where they stood, and the armour was melted, and the men within the armour were not recoverable as men, and [[Honda Katsuhiro]] was at the centre of the line.

Above the bodies the air moved. A figure stood in that garden, tall enough that the eye could not settle upon her height, robed, and crowned, and no thing of this world; and her face was hidden in the manner of faces one has never seen and can nonetheless read. Her hands passed over the dead, and from each man a pale light rose slowly and was gathered into her keeping. She did not speak and did not look at them. The work was already begun when they came into the garden and it was finished with the same patience while they watched. When it was done she set a feather upon the lord's breast and turned and walked away in a manner nothing in that garden walked, and the feather did not move when the wind moved. They took the [[Honda]] crest from the melted armour and held it as such a thing should be held, against rites the city had not yet had time to convene. Later, in a pause in the night's work, the scholars of the court brought them beneath the throne hall, where it is said the castle had always been raised over an earth-node and had always kept the keeping of it quiet. The line was visible to eyes that had begun, in those hours, to read such things. They read it, and set themselves over it, and made the foothold. By then the fires were dying. Dawn was not yet dawn when they came up out of the foundations. The sky was not the sky and the city was not the city, but [[Fukama]] was standing, in the only sense that mattered.
