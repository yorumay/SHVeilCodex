## Of the Audience at Kaisei, and of What Was Done in the Bath

[[Kaisei]] received them with the politeness an occupied city offers strangers it cannot read. They went quietly to rebellious [[Takeda Ren|Ren]] of the clan [[Takeda]] and laid their work before him: the honourable [[Hojo Sadamune|Sadamune]] of the clan [[Hojo]] was to die before they left it. He gave them what they needed — the patrol routes, the shifts upon the gates, the names that would bend and the names that would not — and they came out of his rooms lighter than they had been in a tenday. As they went in, the [[wu jen]] [[Kenji]] was going out. They marked the man, and the hour, and the manner of him. They had themselves been marked.

The summons came within the hour, framed as a courtesy, and refusal would have been a confession, so they went. The [[roju]] received them in a great audience-chamber built for a man who wished to be heard, and took his seat at the centre of it. The display was not subtle and was not meant to be: a [[shogun]]'s cousin, in another man's hall, upon a stolen city, performing the architecture of authority at six travellers with uncertain papers. The audience was theatre and the theatre was practised. He was charming. He read every small lie they offered him and named none of them. What he wanted became visible in time: their hands upon [[Seiryuuyama]], and upon whatever was killing his men on the dragon-mountain's slopes. As warrant of the commission he produced a pearl the size of a heart, milky-yellow with an old fire inside it. _Taken from the dragon by my own hand,_ he said. They asked him for the pearl, saying they would need it upon the slopes, and the asking was as much a test as a request. He gave it. _You should have asked for more,_ he said. They thanked him, and left with the pearl.

[[Saejima Ken]] did not leave with them. A small lizard had travelled into that chamber in [[Ishikui Yondai]]'s pocket, and it left the pocket afterward and went looking, and it found the [[roju]] in his bath, alone. A constrictor came over the rim of the bath and into the water and the water was no longer [[Hojo Sadamune|Sadamune]]'s. He was not, at the last, a soldier; he was a politician with a soldier's face, and he fought as a man fights who does not believe what is happening to him. Twice he nearly broke the hold. He did not break it the third time. The water went dark, and the [[roju]] went down, and the snake went out into the steam.
