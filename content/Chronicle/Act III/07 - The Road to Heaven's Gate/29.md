## Of the Sending at Sakuragawa, and of the Road North

The Sending was convened upon the morning of the third day, when the city had counted enough of its dead to begin. The young daimyo [[Honda Aoi|Aoi]] of the venerable clan [[Honda]] stood at the river's stone steps in white, and her grandmother stood beside her. The wooden tablets had been set out in rows the length of the Sakuragawa's near bank: eighty thousand of them. The figure is written plainly, because the figure is what mattered. Two in every three of those who had been living in that city were written upon them in the days between the fire and that morning. The river went past in the same indifference it has always gone past in. The lanterns were lit one at a time and set upon the water, and went out toward the sea, and took the tablets with them, and the river carried what it always carries. The child spoke. Her words are not written here. It is not the business of a chronicle to keep what a nine-year-old lord says to a city that has just ended.

Of what was said in the streets afterward, this much is set down. The people held that the fire had been a homecoming and not a punishment — that [[Kagutsuchi]] had come back into the world out of the place his father sent him at his birth, and had come seeking that father, and had asked forgiveness of him in the hearing of the whole city. They said also that the dead of that night had not been abandoned, and that hands had come down into the [[Fukiage]] garden to gather them up, and that no soul went unclaimed. Whether the six believed what the city believed, this account does not say. It says only that they did not argue with the grieving. A city in the first week of its ending keeps such structures as it has, and it is not the work of those who saved it to pull them down.

They left upon the third of Mirtul. The northern road out of [[Fukama]] crossed what had been the outer city, drowned now beneath a river gone wider than the canal had ever imagined being, and upon the new bank the [[hai nu]] came up out of the water against them. The fire had hurt the seafolk also, and they had come up to take what could be taken from a country newly weak. The fight was hard, and most of the six were near the bottom of their strength before the end of it. By the time they were across and upon the far shore, [[Fukama]] was a silhouette behind them, and [[Seiryuuyama]] was a thought somewhere ahead.
