## Of the Writ Given at Fukama, and of the Crossing of the Border

They reported to the castle. The venerable [[shugo-daimyo]] [[Honda Aoi|Aoi]] of the clan [[Honda]] heard them from her father's seat, which is still too large for her, and the honorable lady [[Matsudaira Yasuko|Yasuko]] of the clan [[Matsudaira]] heard them standing beside it. They laid the errand out plain: three gods scattered across [[Kozakura]], to be found and brought to a common purpose before the thing the prophecy names. She said that whatever could be done to help them would be provided, and then did the thing that made the sentence real, and had the travel papers drawn — sealed, and stamped, and legitimate. [[Shizume]]'s writ, to be carried out of [[Shizume]].

Then five tendays passed, and the interval is not narrated here, because the interval was fifty days of the work that does not make stories. Forge-time. Market-time. The slow gathering of the right things into the right packs. Blades were reworked, and goods were bought, and other goods sold to buy them, and bodies broken upon a mountain were given the weeks they required. The house held them through it, as it had held them since it was given.

On the twenty-sixth of Kythorn they went out through the northern gate with the papers in hand. The first days gave nothing worth the keeping: good road, good weather, the province going past. On the twenty-seventh the border fell behind them, and this is the first time in the whole of this account that any of them had been outside [[Shizume]]. By evening they were at [[Takeo]], a settlement small enough that its rumour reached them before its roofs did. Something was eating there. The people had no name for it.
