# The Shinzatsu Chronicle
*Being the true and complete account of the six who walked the realms in the years of the breaking, gathered out of every province that remembers them.*

---

What follows is the truth of Shinzatsu, and it is set here whole.

The tellings were scattered when I came to them. In Chibana they are sung; in Kodo they are argued over; in the high country of Takako they are told to children in the winter and are believed by children and by most adults. I have had them from a smith's widow at Renkyu who had them from her husband's mother. I have had them from a temple keeper above Hoshizuki who could give me the whole of the Fifteenth Record and not one word of any other. I have had them from soldiers, and from fisherfolk along the Sakuragawa, and from an old woman at Kumohana who spat when I asked and told me the tale regardless, and gave it more exactly than any other mouth I found. Where a road runs in this country, the tale runs alongside it.

The provinces do not agree, and never have. Tenmei gives the beasts out of their own age at greater length than Shizume gives the fall of a lord. Akime holds that the thri-kreen spoke seven times upon the mountain and Azuma holds that he spoke once. Such quarrels are the ordinary weather of a story that has been carried this far, and I have not been troubled by them. Beneath the quarreling the shape is one shape, and it is the same shape in every province, and that shape is what is written here.

I have set the tellings in their order and made of them a single account, in the belief that a story broken into a hundred keepings is a story on its way to being lost. Each record stands as its own telling and may be read alone, as they were sung alone; read together, they are the whole of it, and the whole of it has not been written down before now.

This is what the realms remember. It is set down entire, that it may be remembered further.
