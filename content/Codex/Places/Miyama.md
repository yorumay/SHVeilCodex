A strategically vital region occupying the narrowest bottleneck of Shinkoku Island, where the mainland narrows to a mere 70 miles across. The province features two spwarling, fertile river plains separated by dense woodlands and the formidable, cloud-shrouded Kurisammyaku Mountains. Its landscape is punctuated by two major bodies of water: the deep waters of Lake Nora-ko and the mist-veiled Lake Uji-ko.

Its administrative heart is the capital of Tamanokuni, which expanded rapidly over the three decades preceding the Bluefire - growing from a modest provincial town into a bustling urban hub boasting well over 20,000 residents. Miyama's economy is heavily anchored in vast rice cultivation, which serves as both the region's main currency and primary tax base. In recent decades, the terraced foothills surrounding the plains have nurtured an emerging tea industry, while the ancient timber of its central woodlands supplies valuable lumber for export across the empire.
